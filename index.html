Google Business Profile suspended? The six common reasons

If Google suspended or disabled your Business Profile, start with the reason it gave you. Google's own help pages describe these six common problems. This page is a free reading list, not a diagnosis of your listing.

The six common reasons

Each reason cites Google Business Profile Help. Read the linked page before you change your profile or file an appeal.

1. The business name does not match the real-world name

Google says your name should reflect your business's real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers. Details such as the address, service area, hours, and category belong in their own fields.

Google also writes: "Including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted, and could result in the suspension of your Business Profile."

Source: Guidelines for representing your business on Google

2. The address is a virtual office, a P.O. box, or a mailbox the business does not operate from

Google's representation guidelines say P.O. boxes or mailboxes located at remote locations aren't acceptable. Create the profile for the actual, real-world location. If the business rents a physical mailing address but does not operate out of that location, also known as a virtual office, that location isn't eligible for a Business Profile.

A co-working space can be listed only if that office maintains clear signage, receives customers at the location during business hours, and is staffed during business hours by your business staff.

Google's eligibility guidelines also list as ineligible "any business whose address is listed as a P.O. box or mailbox in a remote location."

Sources: Guidelines for representing your business on Google; Business eligibility and ownership guidelines

3. The profile does not follow service-area rules

If you serve customers at their locations and do not serve them at your address, Google says to remove or hide the address. For example, a plumber who runs the business from a home should clear the address from the profile.

Service-area businesses should have one profile for the central office or location, with a designated service area. They can't list a "virtual" office unless that office is staffed during business hours.

Google says the boundaries of the overall service area shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time from where the business is based. Quote: "For some businesses, larger service areas may be appropriate." You can set up to 20 service areas by city, postal code, or another type of area. You can't set the area as a radius around the business.

Sources: Guidelines for representing your business on Google; Manage your service areas for service-area & hybrid businesses

4. A listed storefront has no permanent signage, or customers cannot visit during stated hours

Quote: "Businesses showing their address on Google should maintain permanent fixed signage of their business name at the address." If the business does not have permanent on-site signage, Google says it is not eligible as a storefront and should be listed as a service-area business.

To be eligible for a Business Profile, a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours. Google lists a few exceptions, such as ATMs, video-rental kiosks, and express mail drop boxes, plus some rules for seasonal businesses and delivery-only food services.

Sources: Guidelines for representing your business on Google; Manage your service areas for service-area & hybrid businesses; Business eligibility and ownership guidelines

5. There is more than one profile for the same business or location

Google's representation guidelines say: "There should only be one profile per business, as this can cause problems with how your information displays on Google Maps and Search." They also say: "Do not create more than one page for each location of your business, either in a single account or multiple accounts."

If a profile is already suspended or disabled, Google's appeal article says: "Do not create a new Business Profile for the same business."

Sources: Guidelines for representing your business on Google; Fix suspended or disabled profiles

6. The profile does not follow Google's guidelines

Google writes: "We may suspend or disable Business Profiles that don't follow our guidelines."

To try to keep a profile from being suspended, Google requires that you avoid prohibited content and behavior, reflect your business accurately, and comply with the product guidelines. Those guidelines also say to make sure the address and/or service area is accurate and precise, and to "choose the fewest number of categories it takes to describe your overall core business."

This page may not list the exact reason Google sent you. Read the decision in your account and the official guidelines before you appeal.

Sources: Fix suspended or disabled profiles; Guidelines for representing your business on Google

Before you appeal

Use Google's official process. This site does not submit the appeal for you, and we do not promise that Google will restore your profile.

  • Before you submit an appeal, make sure your profile follows all the guidelines.
  • Google's article Fix suspended or disabled profiles explains how to use the official appeals tool. The tool itself is here: Manage appeals for your Google Business Profiles.
  • Google writes: "Appeals can only be submitted once."
  • After a denial, Google says: "Only if your reinstatement request is denied, we may be able to do an additional review to prove your eligibility." That additional review is described here: Request additional review of a denied appeal. You can provide evidence that was not in the original appeal.
  • Optional evidence can include official business registration, a business license, tax certificates, and utility bills. For any document, Google says to check that the business name and address match the profile.
  • If your Google account is restricted, the Business Profiles you manage can be suspended, and you will not be able to create or claim other profiles, according to that article. Google describes a separate account appeal first, then a profile appeal after the restriction is lifted.
